METROVALE METRO — SAFETY AND MAINTENANCE HANDBOOK
Domain: Urban public transportation | Reference period: FY 2025–26

1. PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE
Train inspections occur before the first scheduled departure each operating day. A standard inspection covers brakes,
door interlocks, onboard communication, lighting, and emergency equipment. A train with a failed brake test must not
enter passenger service. Non-critical defects must be logged before the end of the shift and assigned a maintenance
priority.

2. TRACK AND SIGNALING
Track patrols are conducted every 7 days. A detected track defect is classified as critical, major, or minor by the
engineering controller. Critical defects require immediate protection of the affected section and notification to the
Network Control Centre. Signal-system backups are tested on the first Sunday of each month between 00:30 and 03:30,
when passenger service is closed.

3. INCIDENT RESPONSE
For smoke, fire, or suspected electrical arcing, staff must alert the control centre immediately, keep passengers away
from the affected area, and follow the station evacuation plan. Staff must not re-energize equipment until authorized
by the electrical controller. A medical incident should be reported with the station, platform, time, and assistance
requested; personal medical details should be limited to what responders need.

4. ELEVATOR AND ACCESSIBILITY EQUIPMENT
Elevators receive a visual inspection daily and a technical inspection every 30 days. A safety-critical elevator defect
requires the elevator to be taken out of service, signage to be placed at both entrances, and an accessible alternative
route to be communicated. The target repair time is 4 hours, but safety clearance is required before reopening.

5. PERFORMANCE INDICATORS
The annual maintenance target is 98% completion of planned preventive tasks. The target for repeat failures within
30 days is below 3%. In FY 2025–26, planned preventive maintenance completion was 96.5%, and repeat failures were
4.2%. These results indicate a need to improve work-order closure and root-cause analysis.

6. DOCUMENT CONTROL
The Maintenance Manager reviews this handbook every 6 months. Proposed revisions are recorded with a revision date,
owner, and summary of changes. This fictional handbook is a sample dataset for RAG testing, not an operational safety
instruction.
